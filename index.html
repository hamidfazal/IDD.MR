1. Core Framework & Model Shift

  • Terminology Transition: Shift diagnostic terminology from "Mental Retardation" to Intellectual Disability (ID) or Intellectual and Developmental Disability (IDD) to eliminate clinical stigma[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span).
  • Neurodiversity Approach: Structure management around maximizing adaptive functioning, independence, and daily living skills rather than focusing on a baseline "cure[span_5](start_span)"[span_5](end_span).

2. Functional Severity & Etiology Assessment

In low-resource settings lacking advanced genetic or neuroimaging tests, focus clinical evaluation on functional assessment across three primary domains[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span):

Adaptive Domains

  • Conceptual: Communication, functional literacy, basic numeracy, money, and time management[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span).
  • Social: Interpersonal interactions, social evaluation, empathy, and vulnerability to exploitation[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span).
  • Practical: Personal hygiene, dressing, mobility, domestic care, personal safety, and employment[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span).

Functional Severity Classification

  • Mild: Capable of independent living and simple employment with periodic, targeted support[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span).
  • Moderate: Capable of self-care with guidance; needs ongoing family or community support[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span).
  • Severe / Profound: Requires daily intensive support for basic activities of daily living (ADLs)[span_12](start_span)[span_12](end_span).

3. Acute Behavioral Changes: The "HELP" Rule-Out Framework

Do not immediately attribute acute behavioral shifts (e.g., aggression, self-injury, regression) to psychiatric causes or the underlying IDD[span_13](start_span)[span_13](end_span). Systematic physical evaluation should precede behavioral interventions[span_14](start_span)[span_14](end_span).

Domain Reversible Causes to Rule Out First Clinical Action
H – Health (Physical) Dental caries, acute constipation, UTI, GERD, hidden fractures, otitis media, drug side effects[span_15](start_span)[span_15](end_span). Perform physical exam, inspect mouth/ears, check abdomen, run urinalysis/CBC, review drug list[span_16](start_span)[span_16](end_span).
E – Environment Household changes, excessive noise, loss of caregiver, heat stress, schedule disruptions[span_17](start_span)[span_17](end_span). Simplify daily routine, adjust lighting and noise exposure, maintain predictable routines[span_18](start_span)[span_18](end_span).
L – Living Situation Neglect, physical/sexual abuse, exploitation, household conflicts[span_19](start_span)[span_19](end_span). Conduct private screening without family present; check for unexplained bruising or trauma[span_20](start_span)[span_20](end_span).
P – Psychiatric Co-occurring Bipolar Disorder, Major Depression, Anxiety, or Psychosis[span_21](start_span)[span_21](end_span). Evaluate using adapted tools focusing on vegetative signs (sleep, appetite shifts)[span_22](start_span)[span_22](end_span).

4. Management of Co-Occurring Conditions

Epilepsy Co-Management

  • Prevalence: Co-occurs in up to 30% of individuals with IDD[span_23](start_span)[span_23](end_span).
  • Pharmacotherapy: Prioritize broad-spectrum antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) like Valproic Acid or Levetiracetam, which cover multiple seizure patterns and double as mood stabilizers for Bipolar Disorder[span_24](start_span)[span_24](end_span).
  • Behavioral Monitoring: Monitor Levetiracetam for potential worsening of agitation/aggression[span_25](start_span)[span_25](end_span); monitor Topiramate for cognitive slowing[span_26](start_span)[span_26](end_span).

Psychiatric Interventions

  • Start Low, Go Slow: Patients with IDD are uniquely susceptible to extrapyramidal side effects (EPS), excessive sedation, and paradoxical reactions[span_27](start_span)[span_27](end_span).
  • Targeted Use: Reserve psychotropics for specific target symptoms (e.g., severe aggression, extreme agitation), avoiding their use as chemical restraints[span_28](start_span)[span_28](end_span).
  • First-Line Options: Low-dose atypical antipsychotics (Risperidone 0.25–1 mg/day or Aripiprazole 2.5–5 mg/day) when non-pharmacological methods fail[span_29](start_span)[span_29](end_span). Routinely track weight and metabolic markers[span_30](start_span)[span_30](end_span)[span_31](start_span)[span_31](end_span).

5. Caregiver Tool: Behavioral Tracking Log (ABC Log)

For non-verbal or communicatively impaired individuals, behaviors like aggression or self-injury are often functional expressions of unaddressed needs[span_32](start_span)[span_32](end_span). The ABC Log identifies underlying triggers to avoid unnecessary polypharmacy[span_33](start_span)[span_33](end_span).

Components of the ABC Log:
  • A – Antecedent: Events or environmental factors occurring immediately before the behavior[span_34](start_span)[span_34](end_span).
  • B – Behavior: Clear, observable, and measurable actions described objectively[span_35](start_span)[span_35](end_span).
  • C – Consequence: Immediate actions or environmental changes occurring after the behavior[span_36](start_span)[span_36](end_span).

Clinic Log Pattern Analysis

  • Sensory Overload / Escape: Behaviors triggered by noise, crowds, or demands[span_41](start_span)[span_41](end_span). Action: Adapt the environment and simplify tasks[span_42](start_span)[span_42](end_span).
  • Physical Pain: Outbursts recurring at specific times relative to meals, bowel movements, or rest[span_43](start_span)[span_43](end_span). Action: Screen for constipation, GERD, dental caries, or UTIs[span_44](start_span)[span_44](end_span).
  • Tangible/Attention Seeking: Behaviors triggered when items are removed or attention shifts[span_45](start_span)[span_45](end_span). Action: Train caregivers in positive reinforcement strategies[span_46](start_span)[span_46](end_span).
