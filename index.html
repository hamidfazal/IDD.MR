1. Core Framework & Model Shift

  • Terminology Transition: Shift diagnostic terminology from "Mental Retardation" to Intellectual Disability (ID) or Intellectual and Developmental Disability (IDD) to eliminate clinical stigma.
  • Neurodiversity Approach: Structure management around maximizing adaptive functioning, independence, and daily living skills rather than focusing on a baseline "cure."

2. Functional Severity & Etiology Assessment

In low-resource settings lacking advanced genetic or neuroimaging tests, focus clinical evaluation on functional assessment across three primary domains:

Adaptive Domains

  • Conceptual: Communication, functional literacy, basic numeracy, money, and time management.
  • Social: Interpersonal interactions, social evaluation, empathy, and vulnerability to exploitation.
  • Practical: Personal hygiene, dressing, mobility, domestic care, personal safety, and employment.

Functional Severity Classification

  • Mild: Capable of independent living and simple employment with periodic, targeted support.
  • Moderate: Capable of self-care with guidance; needs ongoing family or community support.
  • Severe / Profound: Requires daily intensive support for basic activities of daily living (ADLs).

3. Acute Behavioral Changes: The "HELP" Rule-Out Framework

Do not immediately attribute acute behavioral shifts (e.g., aggression, self-injury, regression) to psychiatric causes or the underlying IDD. Systematic physical evaluation should precede behavioral interventions.

Domain Reversible Causes to Rule Out First Clinical Action
H – Health (Physical) Dental caries, acute constipation, UTI, GERD, hidden fractures, otitis media, drug side effects. Perform physical exam, inspect mouth/ears, check abdomen, run urinalysis/CBC, review drug list.
E – Environment Household changes, excessive noise, loss of caregiver, heat stress, schedule disruptions. Simplify daily routine, adjust lighting and noise exposure, maintain predictable routines.
L – Living Situation Neglect, physical/sexual abuse, exploitation, household conflicts. Conduct private screening without family present; check for unexplained bruising or trauma.
P – Psychiatric Co-occurring Bipolar Disorder, Major Depression, Anxiety, or Psychosis. Evaluate using adapted tools focusing on vegetative signs (sleep, appetite shifts).

4. Management of Co-Occurring Conditions

Epilepsy Co-Management

  • Prevalence: Co-occurs in up to 30% of individuals with IDD.
  • Pharmacotherapy: Prioritize broad-spectrum antiepileptic drugs (AEDs) like Valproic Acid or Levetiracetam, which cover multiple seizure patterns and double as mood stabilizers for Bipolar Disorder.
  • Behavioral Monitoring: Monitor Levetiracetam for potential worsening of agitation/aggression; monitor Topiramate for cognitive slowing.

Psychiatric Interventions

  • Start Low, Go Slow: Patients with IDD are uniquely susceptible to extrapyramidal side effects (EPS), excessive sedation, and paradoxical reactions.
  • Targeted Use: Reserve psychotropics for specific target symptoms (e.g., severe aggression, extreme agitation), avoiding their use as chemical restraints.
  • First-Line Options: Low-dose atypical antipsychotics (Risperidone 0.25–1 mg/day or Aripiprazole 2.5–5 mg/day) when non-pharmacological methods fail. Routinely track weight and metabolic markers.

5. Caregiver Tool: Behavioral Tracking Log (ABC Log)

For non-verbal or communicatively impaired individuals, behaviors like aggression or self-injury are often functional expressions of unaddressed needs. The ABC Log identifies underlying triggers to avoid unnecessary polypharmacy.

Components of the ABC Log:
  • A – Antecedent: Events or environmental factors occurring immediately before the behavior.
  • B – Behavior: Clear, observable, and measurable actions described objectively.
  • C – Consequence: Immediate actions or environmental changes occurring after the behavior.

Caregiver Sample Tracking Template

Date & Time Antecedent (Before) Behavior (Action) Consequence (After) Possible Trigger / Notes
Oct 10, 2:00 PM Asked patient to sit for lunch while loud street music played. Slapped face 3 times and dropped to the floor. Lights turned off, ears covered, lunch delayed. Sensory overload (loud noise) + transition demand.
[Date/Time] [What happened right before?] [Exact behavior observed] [What happened immediately after?] [Notes on pain, environment, or needs]

Clinic Log Pattern Analysis

  • Sensory Overload / Escape: Behaviors triggered by noise, crowds, or demands. Action: Adapt the environment and simplify tasks.
  • Physical Pain: Outbursts recurring at specific times relative to meals, bowel movements, or rest. Action: Screen for constipation, GERD, dental caries, or UTIs.
  • Tangible/Attention Seeking: Behaviors triggered when items are removed or attention shifts. Action: Train caregivers in positive reinforcement strategies.
